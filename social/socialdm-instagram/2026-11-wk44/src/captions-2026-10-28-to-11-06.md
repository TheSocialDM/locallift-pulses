12 new Google reviews in the first three weeks of October.
All five stars. Every one answered within a day.

It's a community we work with in the Southwest, and most of those reviews name someone on the leasing team. That credit belongs to the people on site.

Why does it matter beyond the star rating? Renters tend to read the newest reviews first, and the replies under them. A recent, specific review answers questions a website can't: how patient the team was, how move-in went, what the neighborhood is really like.

In my view, three habits keep reviews coming:

• Asking in person, at a good moment: move-in, a renewal, a fixed work order
• A reply to every review that speaks to what the resident actually said
• Never fishing for five stars. Ask for honest feedback and let the work show.
