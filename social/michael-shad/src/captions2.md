Being seen and being chosen are two different things.

For years, multifamily marketing was about being seen: rank on Google, show up on the listing sites, run the ads. That still matters. But more renters are typing full questions into ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI answers, or asking out loud, like "find me apartments nearby that allow dogs," and getting back a short list of names.

That list is where being chosen starts. These answers lean heavily on what the tools can find and verify about you: your website, your listings, your Google profile and what residents say in reviews. When those sources don't clearly answer the renter's question, another community is more likely to get named.

Three questions worth asking about your property this week:

1. Does your website answer what renters actually ask about, in plain words? Pets, parking, fees, specials.
2. Do your reviews mention real details, or mostly "great place"?
3. Is your Google profile complete and current?

Realtors, here's a shift worth paying attention to.

Buyers and sellers are starting to ask AI tools questions like "who's the best agent for first time buyers in Arcadia?" The answer is usually a short list of names. If you're not on it, that buyer may never hear your name.

AI tools tend to name the agents they can verify and match to the question. That comes from three places:

Reviews that name places and outcomes. "She helped us find our first home in Arcadia" tells AI where you work and who you help. "Great agent!" tells it almost nothing.

A complete Google Business Profile. Service areas, the right categories, real photos and recent posts.

Pages that answer real questions. One neighborhood at a time, in plain language, the way buyers actually ask.

It's the same work that builds trust with people, and now machines read it too.
