Your FAQ page used to be an afterthought. Now it's doing leasing work.

Think about the questions your leasing team answers every day. Do you allow two dogs? Is parking included? What will I actually pay each month? Renters are now asking AI tools those same questions before they ever call you.

When your website answers them clearly, in plain words, AI has something specific to work with. When the answer is buried in a PDF or missing entirely, the renter is more likely to get a vague response, or a competitor whose site spells it out.

A simple place to start this week: list the 20 questions your team hears most, then check whether your website answers each one in a sentence a renter would understand.

The list of options is getting shorter, and that changes things for every local business.

Showing up on page one was the old finish line. Now the finish line is being one of the few names in the answer.

The businesses that get named tend to be the easiest to verify: details that match everywhere they're listed, reviews that describe real experiences, and a website that answers the questions customers actually ask.

AI reads your reviews the way a renter does. It looks for specifics.

A five star review with two words feels good, but it doesn't tell anyone much. A review that mentions a quiet top floor one bedroom, the dog park and maintenance that fixed the AC the same day tells a renter, and an AI tool, exactly what living there is like.

So how do you get more reviews like that?

Ask at the right moments, like just after a maintenance request is closed or a resident renews. Make the ask specific: "What's your favorite part of living here?" invites a real answer.

And reply to reviews, good and bad. Thoughtful replies show future renters how your team treats people.

Three sources. Three different answers. Which one does AI repeat?

Your website says two pets per home. Your Google profile description says one. A listing site says call for policy. A renter asks an AI tool if they can bring both dogs, and the answer they get depends on which source it trusts.

Mismatched details are a common issue, and one of the easiest to fix. Pet policy, pool hours, parking, fees, office hours, even the property name and phone number can drift apart across your website, Google profile and listing sites over time.

Once a quarter, put your key details side by side across every place your community is listed. Pick the correct version and update everything to match.

Do you know what AI says about your property?

Most communities track leads, tours and leases closely. Far fewer check what AI tools say when a renter asks for a recommendation.

Here's a simple monthly test anyone on your team can run. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google these five questions, filled in for your property:

1. Best apartments in [your city]
2. Pet friendly apartments near [your neighborhood]
3. Apartments near [major employer]
4. Apartments with [your top amenity]
5. Is [your community] a good place to live?

For each one, write down whether your community was recommended by name, mentioned in passing, or missing. Do it again next month. The trend matters more than any single answer.
