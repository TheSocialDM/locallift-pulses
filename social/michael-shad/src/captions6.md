The first AI answer is only the start of a renter's search.

The first question is broad: best apartments in Tempe. The AI names a handful of communities. Then the renter keeps going. Which of those allow a large dog? Which have a washer and dryer in the home? Which are closest to campus?

Each follow up narrows the list. A community can make the first answer and drop out by the third, simply because its website never says how big a dog can be or what laundry looks like inside the homes.

This week, ask your leasing team which details make prospects cross a community off their list, like a dog weight limit or laundry inside the home. Then make sure your website states each one as a specific fact, not "call for details."

What dealbreaker comes up most on your tours?

First comment: Agents: more on turning closings into proof: https://www.thesocialdm.com

Your past sales are proof that belongs only to you.

Anyone can write "I know this market." Buyers, and the AI tools they ask, tend to look for evidence. Short write ups of real transactions give them that: the neighborhood, the type of home, the challenge, and how it worked out.

Something like: "Sold a 1950s ranch in Arcadia Lite with a pool that needed work. We priced for the repair up front and had two offers in the first week." (Example only, not a real listing.) One paragraph says where you work, what you handle and how you think. No one else can tell it from your side of the deal.

Pick your last three closings. With your clients' permission, no private details and within your brokerage's advertising rules, write four sentences on each and post them on your website and Google profile.

What's a past sale that taught you the most about your market?

First comment: We fill in Google profiles field by field for communities: https://www.thesocialdm.com

Your Google profile is a fact sheet. How many blanks does it have?

When a renter asks an AI tool about apartments nearby, the answer often leans on business listings, and your Google Business Profile is one of the most visible ones. Every field you fill in is a fact these tools can match to a question.

The fields teams skip most often: relevant additional categories, whatever amenity and accessibility attributes your category offers, holiday hours, and a description that names the neighborhood and what sets the community apart. A profile last touched a year ago tells a renter very little.

Set aside 20 minutes this week. Open your profile in edit mode and go field by field. Anything blank or out of date gets fixed before Friday, including Thanksgiving hours.

Be honest: how many blanks did you count?

First comment: For communities that want more people talking about them: https://www.thesocialdm.com

AI tools tend to notice what other people say about you.

Your website is your side of the story. Many also look for confirmation elsewhere: local news stories, neighborhood guides, employer relocation pages, city event calendars and chamber listings.

When several independent sources describe your community the same way, it becomes easier for an AI tool to repeat that description with confidence. When the only source is your own site, there's less to back it up.

You don't need a PR firm to start. Host a neighborhood event and submit it to local event calendars. Partner with a nearby business. Ask the employers around you whether their relocation pages list nearby housing.

Where has your community been mentioned outside your own website?

First comment: Structured data, checked and kept current for your community: https://www.thesocialdm.com

There's a version of your website only machines read.

Behind the page renters see, a website can carry structured data, often called schema markup. It's a short block of code that labels the facts: this is an apartment community, this is the address, this is the phone number, this is the pet policy.

Visitors don't see it on the page. Search engines like Google use it to understand pages, and it can help other AI tools read each detail correctly instead of guessing. It can cut down on guesswork, though it guarantees nothing about where you show up.

Many property websites have little of it, or have markup that still lists an old phone number. Ask your website provider two questions: what structured data is on our site today, and when was it last updated? You can also paste a page into Google's Rich Results Test to see what it finds.

Has anyone ever walked you through the structured data on your site?

First comment: For agents filming their neighborhoods: https://www.thesocialdm.com

Give your neighborhood videos words AI can read.

A walking tour of a neighborhood can do a lot on video. Some AI tools can process video directly, but many still lean on the text around it. A video with no description, no captions and a title like "Saturday vibes" gives them very little to understand.

So add the words. Give the video a title that names the place, like "Living in Coronado: bungalows and coffee spots." Write a short description of what's in it. Turn on captions and check them, especially street and neighborhood names, which auto captions often get wrong. Then post the video on your website with a few sentences of summary.

Take your best performing video this week and give it a real title, a description and checked captions.

Where do your neighborhood videos live right now, only on social or on your website too?

First comment: Curious how you stack up next door? We map it: https://www.thesocialdm.com

One renter question can put you side by side with the community down the street.

"Community A or Community B if I work from home and need covered parking?" It's an easy question to type into an AI tool. The answer is built from whatever the AI can find about each place: features, fees, reviews, location details.

If your website lists amenities the same way every other site does, a comparison has little to work with. Specific, checkable details give it something: the size and hours of the coworking room, covered parking, package lockers, the walk to the nearest grocery store.

Make a short list of the three communities renters compare you to most. Write down what you offer that they don't, and make sure each one appears on your website in a sentence anyone could verify.

What do renters say when they compare you to the place next door?
