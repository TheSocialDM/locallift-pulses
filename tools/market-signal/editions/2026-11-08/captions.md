## 2026-11-12 | Pillar Communities company page | none
Phoenix renewal rent growth was below zero in Yardi's September report.
A short checklist for reading what that could mean at a single community.

Yardi Matrix's September national report put renewal rent growth in Phoenix at negative 2.4%, and its Phoenix report showed asking rents down 2.1% over the year. A metro figure can point to where to look. Renewal pricing for one community may still need that community's own numbers.

Five things worth reviewing before the next batch of renewal offers:
1. Offer versus advertised: how does each renewal offer compare with the current advertised rent for the same floor plan, specials included?
2. Renewal conversion by floor plan: are residents in one home type leaving more often than others?
3. Cost of a move out: vacant days, make ready and any concession needed to lease the home again.
4. Notice timing: do residents get enough time to decide before they start looking elsewhere?
5. Reasons for leaving: what do move out notes and recent reviews say, beyond price?

If move outs cluster in one floor plan, the answer may sit in that product or its pricing. If they are spread evenly, the resident experience may be the place to start.

A renewal is a second leasing decision.
And your resident may shop it like the first one.

Yardi Matrix's September national report put renewal rent growth in Phoenix at negative 2.4%. Nationally, renewal growth slowed to 1.7%.

What I take from it as a marketer: residents are still renters. When a renewal letter arrives, the comparison may start on a phone, with the listing for their own floor plan and the latest reviews.

That is the gap between being seen and being chosen, applied to the people who already live with you. They already know where you are. Being chosen again may depend on what they find when they look.

Three checks before the next renewal letter goes out:
1. Look up the resident's floor plan on your site and one listing site. How does the advertised price, special included, compare with the renewal offer?
2. Read the five most recent reviews. Are they answered?
3. Does the renewal message say what improved this year, or only the new number?

If the advertised price beats the offer and nobody explains the difference, the first sign you get may be the notice to vacate.

What does your renewal letter say besides the new number?
