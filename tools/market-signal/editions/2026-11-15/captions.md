Most of us read the market through advertised rents.
This fall, the rents Phoenix area residents actually pay moved too.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' latest Phoenix area price index shows the rent index down 1.4% over the two months from August to October. Over the same two months, the Seattle area rent index rose 1.0%.

That index follows what renters in the area pay, existing leases included, not the price on a listing. So it is a different signal from the asking rent reports most of us read every month.

I would not lean too hard on one reading. BLS itself notes that local indexes swing more than national ones, these figures are not seasonally adjusted, and there is no year over year comparison for October because of last fall's gap in federal data collection.

Still, it questions an easy assumption: that softer pricing only shows up in new leases. If it is reaching residents already in place, the rent roll may deserve as much attention as the asking rent report.

The question I would bring to an Arizona operating meeting: are the rents our residents pay moving the same way as the rents we advertise? And in Seattle, where the index rose, does our own rent roll tell the same story?

A metro rent index moved this fall. Did your community move with it?
Five questions to answer with your own numbers before reading anything into it.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports that the Phoenix area rent index fell 1.4% from August to October. The index follows what renters in the area pay, existing leases included, which makes it a different signal from advertised asking rents. It is one reading, not seasonally adjusted, and BLS notes that local indexes move more than national ones. Over the same two months, the Seattle area index rose 1.0%.

Questions a property team can work through before drawing a conclusion:
1. In place rent: how did the community's average rent paid change from August to October?
2. New leases and renewals: which group moved more?
3. Concessions: how much is each special reducing the effective rent?
4. Mix: did more of this fall's leases land in smaller or lower priced floor plans?
5. Nearby communities: are their advertised rents and specials moving the same way?

Read together, these answers may show whether the metro trend is reaching the community or whether the reasons sit closer to home. Either way, the community's own numbers come before any pricing call.
