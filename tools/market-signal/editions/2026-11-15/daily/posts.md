Most of us read the market through advertised rents.
This fall, the rents Phoenix area residents actually pay moved too.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' latest Phoenix area price index shows the rent index down 1.4% over the two months from August to October. Over the same two months, the Seattle area rent index rose 1.0%.

That index tracks what renters in the area are paying, including those on existing leases, rather than the price on a listing. It is a different signal from the asking rent reports most of us read every month.

I would not lean too hard on one reading. BLS itself notes that local indexes swing more than national ones, these figures are not seasonally adjusted, and there is no year over year comparison for October because of last fall's gap in federal data collection.

Still, it questions an easy assumption: that softer pricing only shows up in new leases. If it is reaching residents already in place, the rent roll may deserve as much attention as the asking rent report.

The question I would bring to an Arizona operating meeting: are the rents our residents pay moving the same way as the rents we advertise? And in Seattle, where the index rose, does our own rent roll tell the same story?
