The month in one read

New supply is down from last year. Vacancy has not fallen yet.

RealPage counts 27% fewer U.S. completions through Q3 than a year earlier, yet Census rental vacancy rose to 7.6% in Q3 and Apartment List's index stood at 7.0% in October, below its 7.3% February peak. National asking rents were roughly flat. In the West, vacancy rose year over year and Phoenix's median rent is down 2.5% over 12 months, so national supply relief should not yet be read as local relief.

The Local Reality

  • Phoenix was among the five metros RealPage named for the most Q3 2026 deliveries, each above 3,000 units.
  • Phoenix median rent: $1,250, down 2.5% over 12 months and 0.4% during October; Chandler had the metro's fastest annual growth (0.7%), Surprise the slowest (down 4.7%) (Apartment List).

Limitation: No verified Phoenix or Arizona vacancy or concession figure; Apartment List covers the city of Phoenix, not the metro. No verified Seattle figure.

The Operator Decision

  • Is your vacancy coming from fewer renters finding you, or from more choices near you?
  • Which floor plans carry the most days vacant, and do they have their own photos and pricing online?
  • Do 2027 budgets assume vacancy improves on the national supply timeline or the local one?

Methodology

Figures come from each publisher's own public release and are checked against it before publication. Census vacancy covers all rental housing and carries sampling error; rent measures differ by source, so figures are compared within a source. Local Reality, Operator Decision and interpretation lines are ours, logged in the Signal Ledger with a confidence level. NMHC's October survey is held until its figures can be read on NMHC's own page.
