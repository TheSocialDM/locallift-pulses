The headline this week is that the market moved and you did not. Your net effective rent sits $213 a home per month below the 20-property comp average, where last week the same comparison read $256. That gap closed $43, and almost all of it came from the field cutting its own rent rather than from anything that changed at the property: the comp-set effective rent fell $51 while yours fell $8. The field's average concession jumped from 7.1% to 9.7% over the same window.

Two things we told you last week no longer hold, and both changed because of the field rather than because of you. We said you ran the second-deepest offer of 21. You now run the third, because Sunela's advertised three-months-free finally reached its listed rents and took it from a realized 1.8% to 23.2%. We also said you were the softest property in your own depth band. That band now averages 91.4% leased, which is 1.1 points below you. We are retiring both readings on the face of the report rather than letting them sit quietly next to this week's.

None of that reached the part of the building that actually needs help. Your one-bedrooms ask $77 under the twenty-property average, then take 19.2% off that, and they now average 127 days on market against a 75-day market, up from 122 days last week. Your two-bedrooms ask $50 over the field and clear in 78 days against a 138-day market. One offer, applied flat, across two books moving in opposite directions. Eleven of your listings have now stood past 100 days and ten of them are one-bedrooms.

The most actionable thing in the data this week is smaller and more specific than a pricing decision. Your one-bedroom listing board is not internally consistent. Unit 4029 asks $1,935 for 749 square feet and has stood 269 days. Unit 2017 is the same floor plan at the same 749 square feet, asks $1,630, and has stood 122 days. Both are live right now, $305 apart, against a $1,709 market rent for that plan. We are not claiming price is why either one has sat, because the cheaper of the two has also sat four months. The point is that the asking rents inside a single plan do not line up, and that is an afternoon's work to resolve.

The recommendation is unchanged in direction. Do not withdraw the offer and do not deepen it. Stop paying it on the 123 homes sitting above 95% leased, hold it on the 46 one-bedroom homes carrying real exposure, take the three premium two-bedrooms off it and reprice them, and work the eleven long-standing listings individually. Trimming the tight group to six weeks is worth roughly $79,000 a year and completing the move to four takes it to about $119,000. Both figures apply only to the 42 homes in that group that actually turn in a year, not to the full book. One point on timing: because the market's own effective rent came down, you now reach the field's effective rent at 4.6 weeks free rather than the 3.5 it would have taken last week, so a trim costs you less relative to the field than it did seven days ago.
