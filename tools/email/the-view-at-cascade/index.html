Two things changed in the read this week, and one of them is a correction to what we sent you last week.

Last week's report described the up-to-10-weeks-free offer as a recent tactical move, roughly two weeks old, sitting against a much shallower standing posture. That came off a short data window. The full 30-day pull dates the offer to 5/28. It is 89 days old, it is your standing rate rather than a promotion, and realized concession reads 19.2% on the 30-day basis. We are retiring the earlier read rather than letting it sit quietly alongside this one.

On that longer window the picture is harder. Leased is 92.5%, down 1.1 points and 19th of 21 in the tracked field. Exposure is 10.7% against an 8.4% field average. You signed 4 leases in 30 days against a field average of 13.8. We are not going to tell you the discount caused that, because the field refuses to sort by depth: the fullest property in the market runs an 11.4% offer, the emptiest advertises three months free, and four properties running nothing at all average 4.2 points above your occupancy. What we will say is that at the second-deepest offer of 21, you are the softest property in your own depth band.

The sharper finding is where the softness actually sits. Your one-bedrooms already ask $82 under the field average, then take 19.2% off that, and they still average 122 days on market against a 76-day market. Price level is not what is holding them. Eleven of your twenty live listings have stood past 100 days, nine of those eleven are one-bedrooms, and eight homes carry a June availability date. Meanwhile your two-bedrooms ask $72 over the field and clear in half the market's time. One offer, applied flat, across two books moving in opposite directions.

There is also a structural item that is new this week. Your prices refresh about once every 19 days against a field median of 3.5. Fourteen of the twenty comps run revenue management and reprice every two to four days, and only two properties in the field move slower than you. On a board where eleven listings have stood past 100 days, that cadence is doing more damage than the discount is repairing.

The recommendation is not to withdraw the offer. It is to stop paying it on the 123 homes above 95% leased, hold it on the 46 one-bedroom homes carrying real exposure, take the three premium two-bedrooms off it and reprice them, and work the eleven stale listings individually rather than with another building-wide discount. Trimming the tight group to six weeks is worth roughly $80,000 a year, and completing the move to four takes it to about $119,000. Both figures apply only to the 42 homes in that group that actually turn in a year, not to the full book.

Happy to walk through it live if that is useful.
