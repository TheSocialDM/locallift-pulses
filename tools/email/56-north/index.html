One thing in last week's report needs retiring, and I would rather you hear it from me than spot it. Week 20 made Montreux your benchmark: a quarter mile away, the closest occupancy profile to yours, holding 93.7% leased on no advertised offer at all. Montreux now advertises up to ten weeks free on its next ten move-ins and realises 11.4%. The cleanest argument in last week's edition for holding your rate no longer exists in the form it was made.

That was not an isolated move. This market re-engaged on discounting across the board. Peer realised concession rose from 6.0% to 7.8%, comps publishing no offer at all fell from seven to five, and comps realising under 2% fell from nine to six. One North Scottsdale moved onto a quantified six weeks from none, Regents at Scottsdale went from zero realised to 10.1%, and San Portales jumped from 5.6% to 14.4%. Last week's line that most of this market had stopped discounting is not true this week, and it is retired rather than left standing.

Your own numbers moved the right way. Realised concession eased from 18.9% to 17.6%, third deepest of twenty-one rather than second. Blended net effective rent rose from $1,467 to $1,499, so you are nineteenth of twenty-one rather than last: Regents at Scottsdale and Aria on Cave Creek now both net below you. And the gap between what a new resident signs and what your standing book already earns narrowed for the first time in three editions, from $180 a month to $168.

The core read is unchanged, and it is still the offer. You ask $1,819, fourteenth of twenty-one, and on asking rent per foot you are fourth highest in the whole set. You net $1,499, nineteenth. The obvious objection is unit size, and it does not hold. Read within bed count: your one-bedrooms ask $2.17 a foot against a $2.11 field, 3.0% above it, and net $1.80 against $1.97; your two-bedrooms ask $1.96 against $1.87, 4.9% above, and net $1.60 against $1.74. You price above this market on both lines and net beneath it on the same homes.

Occupancy still went the other way, and one measure moved on its own. Leased fell another 0.8 points to 91.6%, a third consecutive decline, and the gap to the set widened from 3.5 points to 4.1. Exposure is 11.3%, which is 38 homes. The one I would watch next week is marketing time on your one-bedrooms: 110.9 days against an 81.2-day market over thirty days, and over the trailing seven days yours rose 12.3% to 124.6 days while the market held flat. Everything else on the page moved with the market. That did not.

What we are not telling you is that the offer caused the occupancy slip, or that easing it helped. Both moved in the same window and this data cannot order them. Across twenty-one properties, deep and full, shallow and full, and deep and empty are all represented. What we can say without inference is the level: the field realises 7.8% and you realise 17.6%, and six of your twenty comps give away under 2% while holding 94.7% to 100.0% leased.

The move is sequenced and the first step carries almost no risk. Start with B6: 66 homes, your second largest plan, 100% leased at 1.5% exposure, turning in 41 days, and its own row still carries $221 a month of offer, the largest dollar offer on any plan you have at scale. Pulling it there is worth about $87,500 a year across the homes that turn. Then step the property offer from 9.2 realised weeks to eight, about $74,000, and to six once exposure prints under 9%, taking the cumulative figure to about $202,400. Six weeks is still the deepest tier that is common here, occupied by IMT Desert Ridge next door, One North Scottsdale, The Catherine Townhomes, Desert Club and GlenEagles. Every one of those figures applies to the 153 homes that turn in a year at your 55.5% blended renewal rate, never to all 344.

The softness is in two plans, not across the book. Open 1 Bedroom sits at 15.8% exposure, 203 days and the lowest effective rent you have at $1,412. B2 sits at 18.9% exposure. Between them they hold thirteen of your thirty-eight available homes on 21% of your unit count. On volume you signed ten leases against a field averaging 11.5, twelfth of twenty-one, so you are signing at roughly the market's pace. What separates you is the price those ten signed at.

Two published fee lines are still simply empty. You publish no application fee where sixteen of twenty comps publish one averaging $59, and no admin fee where eight publish one averaging $202, worth roughly $30,900 a year across the homes that turn. Worth confirming both are genuinely absent rather than just unsurfaced before acting; if you already charge them, the opportunity is in the listing copy instead. And your amenity count is a real strength: nine of the ten tracked building amenities against a field averaging 7.4, joint second of twenty-one. Lead the listings with that rather than with free weeks.
