The headline this week is a divergence. Across the last seven days of the reporting window your offer ran at its full advertised depth, 19.2%, which is exactly what up to ten weeks free prices out at. Over those same seven days the tracked market moved the other way and pulled its concessions back by about a fifth. Slate Scottsdale withdrew its four-week offer entirely, and San Portales re-posted at six weeks rather than eight. Across the full 30 days you realised 16.6%, the second deepest of the twenty-one properties we track.

Occupancy did not follow. Leased slipped 0.9 points to 93.3% and exposure rose 0.6 to 11.3%, now the second highest in the set behind only Sunela. Measured across the twenty comps with your property excluded, the set sits at 96.2% leased and 7.1% exposure. To be careful about what this shows: both things moved inside the same window, and the data cannot tell us the deeper offer caused the slip. What it does establish is a level. At the second deepest offer in the set your occupancy is the second softest, and every competitor running a shallower measured discount holds more occupancy than you do.

Your rent is not the problem, and that is the part worth holding on to. Per square foot you ask $2.07 against a $1.99 set average, four percent above it, and you are above the set on both bed counts. The blended dollar comparison looks far worse than that because your homes average 872 square feet against 981 and you carry no studios and no three-bedrooms. Size-adjusted, your effective rent sits 8.5 percent below the set rather than 18.7 percent, and all of that residual is the concession.

One correction to last week's edition, which you are still holding. Last week's report said your homes sat 111 days on market against a 106-day set average and that you were slower than the set. Marketing time has improved to 101 days, so measured against the average the set publishes, that claim no longer holds and it is withdrawn. Measured against the median it does still hold: the typical tracked property turns a home in 81 days, and fifteen of the twenty turn faster than you. The full list of what changed is on the face of the report.

The recommendation is sequenced rather than a single cut. Hold your asking rent. Take the offer from ten weeks to eight now, worth about $130,000 a year across the roughly 156 homes that turn, then to six once exposure prints below nine percent, which takes it to about $260,000. Point whatever offer remains at Open 1BR and A4, which carry 200-day and 140-day marketing times, rather than at B6, which is fully leased and turns in 45 days. And take the admin fee and covered parking income now, about $56,000 a year that no renter comparing listings will ever see.
