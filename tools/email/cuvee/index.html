I am reversing what your last edition told you, and I would rather say so in the first line than bury it. That edition, dated September 6, said the market had moved up to your offer level and that stepping your discount down was the wrong move. The market has moved back. The field's realised discount went from 14.8% to 11.9%, yours went from 18.3% to 18.2%, and the premium you are paying alone doubled from 3.5 points to 6.3. There was no September 13 edition for Cuvee, so this is a two-week comparison rather than a one-week one, and the report says so on its face.

The number I would actually decide on is not the discount, it is the volume. You signed 7 leases across the 30-day window against a field averaging 14.5, which is tenth of eleven. Agave Ranch signed the same 7 leases on a 2.4% discount, and the three properties in the report paying between 2.4% and 3.4% signed 46 between them. I want to be careful here: the field's volume fell too, from 17.9 per property to 14.5, so part of this is the market rather than you, and three properties is not proof that discounting less produces volume. What the data does support is narrower and still decisive. Your depth is not buying pace.

Your price position is the strongest in the set, and that is not the problem. You ask $1.85 a square foot, first of eleven, and after the offer you net $1.52 against a field at $1.48, fourth of eleven. Your homes average 840 square feet against 946, so the per-foot line is the honest comparison and the blended dollar figure understates you. The whole distance between first on asking and fourth on net is the concession. There is no case here for cutting your asking rent.

Occupancy turned this fortnight, for the first time in three editions. Leased fell from 94.2% to 91.6%, exposure rose from 14.5% to 15.5%, and available homes went from 45 to 48. Days on market went from 116 to 131 against a field that went from 93.5 to 92, so unlike the prior edition that move is on your side of the comparison. Only one property in the whole set is slower than you.

The work is 68 one-bedroom homes and it costs nothing in rent. Bordeaux A1 is the only one-bedroom plan you own priced at or under the one-bedroom market on both dollars and per square foot, at $1,379 and $1.76 against a market at $1,425 and $1.84. On 119 homes it runs 97.5% leased at 53 days. The other 68 one-bedroom homes are above the market on one measure or both, and they run 162 days at 32.3% exposure. Petit Verdot A6 is the biggest single piece of that: 29 homes, 205 days, $2.22 a foot against a $1.84 market. Bringing that group to your own book average is worth about $144,300 a year across the 41 of them that turn. That is the first move, and it does not touch the offer.

Then walk the discount, in steps, and gate it. Closing the full 6.3 points to the field is worth about $213,100 a year across the 181 homes that turn in a year at your 41.6% blended renewal rate, never across all 310. I would take it one to two points at a time and read the lease count each week, because volume is the thing the depth was bought to protect. And I would gate it to the one-bedroom book: your two-bedrooms are 96.7% leased at 9.8% exposure and lease 11% faster than their market, and right now they carry the same discount as the line running 77% slower than its market.

Two notes on the report itself, so nothing on it surprises you. Resident ratings are back in this edition after being held out while the profiles were re-verified. Yours reads 3.1 stars against a field at 4.0, tenth of eleven. Set against that, your finish score is 9 of 10, joint best in the set, and you carry all ten tracked building amenities, so the product is not what is holding this back. Second, Alexan Park West sits in Peoria rather than Glendale, 1.15 miles out, and it stays in the set because it competes for the same renter; the report names it rather than folding it in quietly.
