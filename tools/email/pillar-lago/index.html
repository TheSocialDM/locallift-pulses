One line moved the wrong way and it is the reason for this edition. Your seven-day moving median listed rent fell 10.4% across the window while the market's rose 0.3%. Last edition the same pair read down 3.6% against a market up 1.7%, so the gap between you and the market went from about four points to about eleven in a week. A median moves with which homes are listed as well as with what they are priced at, so we are not going to convert that into a dollar figure or call all of it a rate decision. What we will say is that nothing in this report suggests you needed it. Our first recommendation is to hold your asking rents where they are for two weeks and re-measure.

On the offer, one thing has changed since last week and it changes our advice. Your realised concession now reads 11.5% against a field that realises 11.1%, so there is no meaningful walk to the market left to make. Last week's report put the remaining walk from six weeks to four at about $56,000 a year, and we are retiring that figure on the face of the report rather than quietly dropping it. The 11.5% is exactly the face value of six weeks free, so your measured offer has converged on your advertised one. We are not asserting a cause for the change, because the data does not support one.

What we would do instead is restrict the six weeks rather than shorten it. Your offer is advertised on all homes; seven of the eighteen live offers in this field restrict to select homes. Your one-bedrooms clear in 18 days against a 102-day market and are fully leased. Your two-bedrooms already realise 5.9% against a 9.9% market and clear in about half the market's time. Between them that is 88 of your 120 homes that need nothing. The Haven is the exception: 90.6% leased, 9.4% exposure, 115 days against a 91-day market, and the only line running a deeper offer than its own market. That is where the six weeks should be concentrated, and it is a product and positioning question rather than a price one, because the deeper discount there has not bought pace.

If the property blended at the 5.9% your own two-bedrooms already achieve, that is about $83,700 a year, or $109 per home per month, measured against the sixty-four of your hundred and twenty homes that actually turn in a year at a 46.7% blended renewal rate, not against the full book.
