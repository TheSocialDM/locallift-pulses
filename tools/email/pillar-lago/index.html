You net $1,743 against a field average of $1,520, fifth highest of the twenty-one properties we track. One honest qualifier on that premium: in dollars it reads 14.7%, but your homes average 1,016 square feet against the field's 942, so per square foot the gap is 6.2%. Roughly half of the headline is home size rather than rent, and we would rather tell you that than quote you the bigger number.

Here is this week's finding. Of your five available homes, two are three-bedrooms that have now been listed 142 and 192 days. They are effectively your whole exposure problem. What makes them worth acting on is where the gap actually sits: those two homes already net within $50 of their own market, at $1,902 and $1,946 against a modelled $1,950 for that plan. What sits above the market is the advertised rent, $2,150 and $2,200, against a $2,056 three-bedroom market. You are already giving the money back through free weeks, in a place no renter filtering a search by price will ever see it.

That changes the advice we gave you last time, and we would rather say so plainly than quietly move on. Last edition recommended restricting the six weeks to The Haven, on the reading that the three-bedroom was the one plan that needed the offer. Since then that plan's realised offer has gone from 8.2% to 11.0% against a three-bedroom market sitting at 5.1%, and its time on market has gone from 115 days to 129 while the market's fell to 81. The offer went where we said it should go and the plan got slower. We are not claiming the offer caused that, and the data will not support a claim either way. What we can say is that the offer lever has now been tried on this plan and the asking rent has not.

So the recommendation is to move the three-bedroom ask toward the $2,056 market and cut the offer on it from 11.0% to about 5.1% at the same time. On the arithmetic that nets you roughly $22 per home per month more than today while advertising $111 less. The money is not really in the rent, it is in the time: closing that 47.8 day gap to the market's pace is worth about $39,300 a year across the thirteen of your thirty-two three-bedrooms that actually turn in a year at that plan's 59.4% renewal rate, not across all thirty-two and not across the full book.

Leave the other two plans alone. The Alcove is 100% leased with zero exposure and there is nothing there to discount. The Retreat is 97.9% leased and its offer is already level with its own market, 10.3% against 10.2%. Between them that is 88 of your 120 homes that need nothing at all. One genuinely encouraging note on the three-bedroom: it carries your highest renewal rate at 59.4% and the only positive trade out in your book. The people living in those homes are re-signing at or above their expiring rent. The problem is confined to filling them from cold.

Two housekeeping notes. Eighteen of the twenty comps publish a one-time pet fee averaging $279 and your listings show none. If you charge one and it simply is not reaching your listings, that is free money and about ten minutes of work, so it is worth checking. And we want to flag something before it surprises you next week: across the last seven days your realised offer on both the two-bedroom and the three-bedroom reads 1.6%, down sharply, and the market pulled back in the same week. We are not going to tell you what changed in the listings because we cannot see it from here. Next week is the first window that can measure it properly.
