Last week I wrote that the field had repriced hard and that the move was not to match it. Ten days later that wave has largely unwound, and the more interesting finding is inside your own book.

The market pulled back. Seventeen of your twenty comps now advertise a concession, against nineteen last week, and realised offer depth across the field has halved from 14.5% to 8.8%. Two of the properties that posted offers in early September have already withdrawn them, and both are inside a mile: Borrego at Spectrum has gone from 13.0% realised to 0.0% while holding 96.6% leased, and Elevation at the Village from 14.7% to 4.2% at 98.1% leased.

You went the other way. Your advertised offer moved from four weeks to six and your realised depth from 7.7% to 8.5%. Because the field retreated at the same time, a 6.8 point offer advantage is now 0.3 points, and you have gone from third-shallowest of twenty-one properties to eleventh. Your premium per square foot narrowed from 30% to 20.5%, and about two thirds of that is the field rising rather than you easing. I am not going to tell you the extra two weeks bought the 0.7 points of occupancy you gained; thirteen leases in a window will not support that claim either way.

The position itself is still excellent. You are 98.7% leased at 5.7% exposure, second and fourth of twenty-one. You signed 13 leases, exactly the field average, out of roughly half the field's available inventory. You net $1.94 per square foot against a field at $1.61, on homes 12% smaller. New leases are signing about $149 above your modeled in-place book.

The finding worth your time is the trade-out split, and it is the clearest it has been. Your book divides in two and the halves move in opposite directions. All three two-bedroom plans re-lease above the expiring rent: B2 at +4.31%, B3 at +4.10%, B5 at +4.12%. All five studio and one-bedroom plans re-lease below it, from -2.33% to -5.59%. That is 109 homes up and 188 homes down, an 8.1 point spread, with no plan on the wrong side of the line.

So the recommendation is the same one for a fourth week, and this week I reverified it rather than carrying it forward. Take the blanket offer off the two-bedrooms. They renew at 55.1%, the highest in your book. They clear in 30 days. They are the only plans re-pricing upward. And they now hold nine of your seventeen available homes, more than the other 188 homes put together. Taking the offer off that line is worth about $104,000 a year across the 49 two-bedrooms that turn. The condition I set last week for revisiting the call, two-bedroom exposure climbing with days on market climbing alongside it, did not happen: exposure held at 8.3% and days to lease held at 30.

Two readings from last week are retired on the face of the report. The first is the premium, which read 30% and now reads 20.5%. The second is the studios: I have called them the soft spot in your book for three weeks, and this week they cleared. Studio availability fell 44% in seven days, you leased 9 studios in the window against a market average of 4, and four studio homes remain. Keep their offer one more week, then re-check it.

One thing to put on next week's agenda. Plan A1, 39 homes, re-leases 5.59% below the expiring rent and sits at zero exposure, fully leased with nothing on market. No occupancy report will ever surface that, because there is nothing to show. Across the studios and one-bedrooms the same pattern is giving back roughly $63,000 a year on the homes that turn. That is the quiet cost of holding 98.7%, and it is where I would go once the two-bedroom offer is settled.
