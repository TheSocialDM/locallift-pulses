I need to reverse last week's call, and I would rather say so plainly than let it sit. Last week I told you the offer gap had closed and there was no cut left to make. This week your concession across the window is 14.2% against a market at 8.5%, and you are the fourth deepest discount of the twenty-one communities in the report. That is not a small revision and I am not going to dress it up.

The important part is what did not change. Your advertised offer is exactly what it was: up to 8 weeks free on 12 to 13 month terms, live three days. What moved is how much of it your listings are actually pricing. A 14.2% window concession is 7.4 of those 8 weeks, and last week the same headline was pricing at 2.4. I am not going to tell you why that happened, because the advertised number did not move and the data cannot carry the explanation. What I can tell you is the level, and the level is that you are now giving away close to the full advertised offer.

It cost you the premium. You still ask $2.36 a square foot against a $2.17 market, unchanged, and you are fifth of twenty-one on asking. But you now net $2.03 against $2.01, which is 1.0% ahead and ninth of twenty-one, where last week you were 8.1% ahead and fourth. On a simple average of the twenty rows the set nets $2.04, which would put you marginally below, and I am giving you both bases because they disagree this week. Nothing about your product or your asking rent changed. The discount did.

The corridor went the other way. Four of last week's deepest discounters pulled back hard: one fell from 15.1% to 1.4%, another from 14.2% to 2.8%, another from 10.9% to 2.1%, another from 8.9% to 3.7%. Two of the five communities that were advertising deeper than you last week now advertise no rent offer at all. Twelve of your twenty comps are promoting something today and eight are promoting nothing.

Here is the number I would act on. Your one-bedroom rows are giving back $166 per home per month. Across the 93 one-bedroom homes that actually turn in a year at their 48.9% renewal rate, that is about $185,000, roughly three quarters of the entire modelled cost of your offer. That line is 182 of your 227 homes, it is 98.9% leased, and it leases in 20.9 days against a 72.3 day market. On the trailing seven days its concession ran 15.4% against a market at 8.9%. You are spending most of the offer on the homes that were going to lease anyway. Gate it by plan, by term and by move-in date.

The two-bedroom line I have been leading with for three weeks actually improved. Sixteen homes, now 81.2% leased with 25.0% available, at 217.8 days against a 105.7 day market, against 75.0%, 31.3% and 220.7 last week. Encanto filling from 83.3% to fully leased accounts for all of it. Palmcroft is still the sharp end: all three homes available, no renewals, 188 days on market. Closing the remaining gap is worth about $80,200 a year across the 9 two-bedroom homes that turn at a 43.7% renewal rate, or about $49,600 measured on the in-place rent roll instead. It is no longer the biggest number in your report.

Two more things. Your studios went the other way this week, 82.8% leased against 89.3%, with 17.2% available, so that is the line to watch. And all seven of your floor plans still carry a negative trade-out, essentially flat at -3.68% while the offer tripled, which is precisely why I will not connect those two.

On the review count I promised you last week: it is still not in this week's data, so I am not going to put a number in front of you that I have not checked. What I can tell you is that your rating is 4.7 stars and Roosevelt Row rose to match it this week, so you are joint highest of the twenty-one rather than alone at the top. One correction while I am here: the 3.4 set average I quoted last week was the source's first block of ten comps rather than the whole set. Across all twenty it is 3.6. The two asks stand either way and should be habit by now: one at every renewal conversation, one every time a work order closes.
