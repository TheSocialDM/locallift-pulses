The short version: your offer did not come down, the market's did. You are still running 50% off four months, which is 16.7%, the same offer as three weeks ago. Over the same period the comp set walked its average down from 9.8% to 8.2%, and seven of the twenty communities we track now run no offer at all, up from four. The headline table on the report shows your window average at 12.4% rather than 16.7%, and that is worth understanding before it gets read as a cut: the current offer was recorded from 19 August, so the 30 day average blends a lighter first half of the window with what you are running now. Nothing about your offer has been reduced.

The pricing case has not changed, and it is still a good one. You ask $2.37 a square foot against a $2.20 market, 7.7% ahead, on a building that is 96.5% leased with the highest resident rating of all 21 properties in the set at 4.7 stars. Please read the raw dollar comparison with care: your homes average 694 square feet against 770 across the set, so the $141 gap between your $1,434 blended net effective rent and the $1,575 set average is floor plan, not underpricing. Per square foot you are ahead on asking rent and ahead on net.

What the offer costs. On the window average you net $2.07 a foot against a $2.04 market, so 6.3 of your 7.7 points of asking premium are consumed by the concession. At the 16.7% offer standing today the same asking rent nets $1.97 a foot, which is below the market. Walking the offer back to 8.2% is worth about $101,000 a year, and if today's offer runs a full window the same walk is worth about $203,000. Both figures apply only to the 121 homes that turn in a year at your 46.7% blended renewal rate, not to all 227, because a home that renews does not re price.

The one thing I would do first is free. Your advertised offer and your realised offer are the same number, which means every signer takes it, on every plan and every term. Half the corridor gates its offer by term, by plan or by move in date and realises a fraction of what it advertises: one nearby community advertises ten weeks free and realises nothing at all. Gating costs you no headline and no rent. It also puts the incentive where it belongs, because your one bedroom core is 182 of your 227 homes, runs 98.8% to 100% leased and absorbs faster than the market, while the 16 two bedroom homes sit at 170 days against a 105 day market. A blanket offer spends the most money exactly where absorption is strongest.

One thing to keep an eye on rather than act on this week: all seven of your floor plans now carry a negative trade out, against six of seven three weeks ago. Occupancy is holding while realised rent slips. I would not attribute that to the offer, since the market's own offer moved over the same window, but it is the number I would watch as you trim.
