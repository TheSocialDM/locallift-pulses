Why we keep raising this one

What we watch a review actually do

Two effects, and they are different. In almost every lease-up we report on, the rating is not the problem. The count is, and the only thing the communities ranked above you have that you do not is time. This is the one lever we cannot pull from our side, which is the honest reason it keeps showing up on your report.

It moves where they sit on the map

Map position comes from three things: how close the renter is, how well the listing matches what they typed, and how established the community looks. Review count and rating sit inside that third one. Distance cannot be changed and we handle the listing, so on a new building the review base is the part that is still moving.

It is the sentence AI repeats

When a renter asks an assistant which community to pick, it does not read the brochure. It reads what residents wrote, and it quotes them almost word for word. A community with eleven reviews gives it almost nothing to say. That is why two buildings with the same rating can get described completely differently.

What is actually producing reviews right now

Nine moments a resident is already happy

The pattern across every community we watch is the same. Nobody writes a review because they got an email. They write one because somebody asked them in a moment when they felt looked after. On a lease-up almost every resident is inside their first ninety days, which is the most generous window a community ever gets, and it closes. Here is each moment, what we see it do, and one sentence your team is welcome to use as is.

Moment 01

The first week after move-in

The single best window in the whole resident relationship, and on a lease-up nearly everyone is in it right now. They chose this place over four others last month and they still feel good about the decision. Wait six months and that feeling is gone.

One line"You picked us over a lot of options. If you would not mind saying why in a couple of sentences, it genuinely helps the next person who is deciding."
Moment 02

The minute a work order is closed

The highest-yield ask in the business and the most underused. Somebody came into their home and fixed their problem. Gratitude is at its peak for about an hour, and then it is over. The tech is the one who should ask, not the office, because the tech is the one they are grateful to.

One line"Glad that is sorted. If you have thirty seconds, a quick review means a lot to our team, and you can mention me by name if you want."
Moment 03

A package or delivery saved

Somebody's parcel went missing, or got delivered to the wrong door, or came while they were away and the office kept it safe. Small problem, disproportionate relief. Renters talk about package handling constantly, and almost nobody has reviews that mention it.

One line"Found it. Honestly, if package stuff is something you care about, that is worth putting in a review, people ask us about it on every tour."
Moment 04

Parking finally sorted

A spot reassigned closer to their door, a garage fob that stopped working, a neighbor parked across two spaces, guest parking sorted for a visitor. Parking is the thing residents complain about most, so a resident who is happy about parking is unusually motivated to say so.

One line"That is handled. If you have a second, mentioning the parking in a review would help us, it is the first question half our tours ask."
Moment 05

The first time they use an amenity

On a new building the pool, the gym, the dog run and the lounge are the reason a lot of them signed. Catching someone walking out of the gym at seven in the morning, or leaving the dog park, is catching them at the exact moment the building is delivering what it sold.

One line"How is the gym treating you? If you like it, that is the kind of thing worth saying in a review, people cannot tell from photos."
Moment 06

Resident events

Coffee in the lobby, a food truck, a pool morning, a holiday thing. Everyone is in one place, in a good mood, with their phone in their hand and five minutes to spare. One printed card on the table beats a thousand emails. This is the only moment on this list where asking ten people at once is natural.

One line"Glad you came. There is a card on the table if you feel like leaving us a review while you are standing here, it takes about a minute."
Moment 07

When somebody says thank you out loud

The one that costs nothing and gets skipped every single day. A resident says "you guys are great" at the mailbox, in the hallway, in the elevator. Most teams say thank you and move on. The whole idea here is that someone on the team is allowed to answer that with a question.

One line"That means a lot. Would you mind putting that exact thing in a review? What you just said is better than anything we could write."
Moment 08

The tour that turned into a lease

Somebody just chose this community while it was still fresh against everywhere else they saw. They know exactly what made the difference, and in two months they will not remember. A lease-up gets dozens of these a month and almost nobody asks.

One line"Welcome in. While it is fresh, what made you pick us over the other places you looked at? That is exactly the thing to put in a review if you have a minute."
Moment 09

Anybody who sends a friend

A resident who refers someone has already recommended the community out loud to a person they care about. Asking them to write down what they already said is the smallest ask on this page, and it is the review most likely to name a specific person or floor plan.

One line"Thank you for sending them our way. You clearly already say nice things about us, any chance you would write a couple of them down?"

The difference we can see in the data

What makes one review worth three

A five star review with no words still lifts the average, and does nothing else. A five star review with the right words gets quoted back to renters for years. Steering it costs your team nothing.

Reviews that do very little

"Great place to live."

"Love it here, highly recommend!"

"Five stars."

Nothing in these can be quoted to a renter who is deciding, and nothing in them matches what people actually type when they are searching.

Reviews that keep working

"Marco in maintenance fixed our AC the same afternoon we called."

"We are in a two bedroom with the balcony and the natural light is the reason we picked it."

"Ten minutes from work and the dog park actually gets used."

A name, a floor plan, an amenity, a neighborhood. These are the sentences that get repeated back to the next renter.

How to steer it without scripting anyone Nobody needs to be told what to write. Just ask about the thing that actually happened. If you fixed their sink, say mention the maintenance. If they are in love with the two bedroom, say mention the floor plan. People write back whatever you put in their head.
Our half of it, and one warning worth having

What we carry, and what to stay away from

The second list is the one we would want to be told about. We have watched communities lose reviews they had already earned this way.

We handle this so nobody has to remember
  • A short link and a code they can scan, sized for a work order card, an event table, a move-in folder and an email signature. They never type anything.
  • A reply to every review, good or bad, in the community's voice, so the team is not writing them at nine at night.
  • The count and rating on every report, against the communities ranked above you, so progress is visible instead of a feeling.
  • The review language fed back into your listing, so the words residents actually use end up where renters are searching.
Three things that can cost you the reviews you already have
  • Never offer anything for a review. No gift card, no raffle, no rent credit, no pizza. It is against the rules on every platform, and reviews bought that way get removed in batches later, usually the good ones.
  • Never screen first. Asking who is happy before deciding who gets the link is against the rules too. Ask everyone in the moments above and the screening takes care of itself.
  • Never blast the whole community at once. A pile of reviews landing in one week from one building looks exactly like the thing platforms filter out. Steady and in person beats a campaign every time.
If you only take one thing off this page

The smallest version that works

Ignore everything above and do this. It is four lines, and it is the version that survives a busy week on a lease-up.

  1. One person owns it. Not the team, one named person, usually whoever runs move-ins. Shared ownership means nobody does it.
  2. Two moments, not nine. Move-in week and closed work orders. Those two alone cover most of a lease-up's residents in any given month.
  3. Ask out loud, in person, once. No follow up, no chasing, no second email. If they say no, that is the end of it.
  4. We do the rest. The link, the card, the replies, and the counting. All they own is the sentence.
That is the whole thing Two moments, one person, one sentence. Do that for thirty days and we will show you exactly what it did to your position on next month's report. If it works here the way it has worked elsewhere, you will see it before the quarter is out.
